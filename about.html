02 The Path

How I got here

It started with music

My path into web development was not especially direct.

Late 80s and onward

Before web development became my career, music was the thing I spent most of my time pursuing. I started playing drums in bands in the late 1980s and spent years performing in both original and cover bands.

It never became the way I paid the bills, but it gave me a lot of great experiences. It also taught me how to listen, collaborate, adapt, and keep working at something until all the pieces come together.

Those lessons have carried over into the way I approach development.

03 The Work

More than two decades on the web

Building things people depend on

I enjoy the point where design, front-end development, accessibility, and real business needs meet.

Over the years, I’ve worked on newspaper websites, client projects, high-volume subscription pages, internal tools, and full-stack applications.

A lot of my professional work has involved improving existing systems rather than starting from scratch. That means understanding the business rules already in place, finding practical ways to modernize the experience, and making changes without breaking what people depend on.

04 What Matters

What I care about

Useful work, done carefully

I like interfaces that are easy to understand and code that another developer can follow.

01

Accessible by default

Semantic structure, keyboard access, clear feedback, usable forms, and interfaces that do not leave people guessing.

02

Responsive and dependable

Layouts and interactions should work across devices and remain reliable when the content or conditions are less than ideal.

03

Readable code

I value clear structure, sensible naming, reusable patterns, and code that another developer can understand later.

04

The whole system

I like understanding how the interface connects to APIs, authentication, databases, testing, and deployment.

Whether I’m working on an interface, learning a new framework, playing drums, or trying to improve an edge, I’m drawn to the same things: craft, problem-solving, and the satisfaction of getting the details right.
